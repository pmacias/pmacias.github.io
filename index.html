Clickworthy (in progress)

Clickworthy re-analyzes 3,823 real headline A/B tests from the Upworthy Research Archive to ask how much a typical test could actually tell you. Classical checks show the median test could only detect a lift of about 83%, and a simulation shows that peeking at live results raises the false-positive rate from 5% to 25%. A hierarchical Bayesian model in PyMC then pools evidence across all tests, pulling noisy "winners" back toward realistic effect sizes. Causal inference is next.

Flashpoint

Flashpoint predicts whether an active wildfire will escalate or stay contained, using only the first one to two days of fire and weather data. It combines weather, topography, and vegetation features in an Explainable Boosting Machine, reaching 0.75 macro-F1 on the binary escalation task against 0.42 for a majority-class baseline, and catching 95% of fires that went on to escalate. XGBoost is kept as a secondary robustness check.

Pivot Quiz

Coming from academia, I needed a way to quickly understand the current tech landscape as it is constantly evolving. Realizing there was no readily available tool beyond multiple choice quizzes and paywalls, I built Pivot, which uses Claude through API calls and uses scaffolding to build signal (work style, skills, collaboration level) and suggest roles in tech and help identify and mitigate skill gaps.

NASA Roman Space Telescope

I helped design the High Latitude Time Domain Survey (HLTDS) for NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, a flagship mission that launched in August 2026. The survey design our team recommended beat NASA's dark-energy requirement and became the basis for the survey's observing strategy.

As a postdoc at UC Santa Cruz and a member of a Science Investigation Team, I ran and analyzed about 500 simulated survey campaigns, comparing candidate designs (depth, cadence, filters) on a single figure-of-merit. The survey itself is scheduled to begin in late 2028. Learn more about it at the link below.
