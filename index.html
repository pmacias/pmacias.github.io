Flashpoint

Can the first two days of a wildfire tell you whether it will escalate?

SkillsGradient boosting (XGBoost) · Explainable Boosting Machines · convolutional neural networks with Grad-CAM · leave-one-year-out cross-validation · feature ablation · large raster data pipelines (HDF5, DuckDB)

Flashpoint predicts whether an active wildfire will escalate or stay contained, using only the first one to two days of fire and weather data. It combines weather, topography, and vegetation features in an Explainable Boosting Machine, reaching 0.75 macro-F1 on the binary escalation task against 0.42 for a majority-class baseline, and catching 95% of fires that went on to escalate.

XGBoost with GFS forecast features edges it on macro-F1, but the EBM is kept as primary for its higher escalation recall.

Clickworthy

How much can a typical headline A/B test actually tell you?

SkillsA/B testing · statistical power and minimum detectable effect · multiple-testing correction (FDR) · simulation of peeking at live tests · Bayesian hierarchical modeling (PyMC, MCMC)

Clickworthy re-analyzes 3,823 real headline A/B tests from the Upworthy Research Archive to ask how much a typical test could actually tell you. Classical checks show the median test could only detect a lift of about 83%, and a simulation shows that peeking at live results raises the false-positive rate from 5% to 25%.

A Bayesian hierarchical model in PyMC then pools evidence across all tests, pulling noisy "winners" back toward realistic effect sizes: among the 382 tests a naive read would call significant, the median winning lift drops from +64% to +45%. Causal inference is next.

Spirometry

How much do buffs and nerfs affect card pick rates in a deckbuilder?

SkillsCausal inference · natural experiments · difference-in-differences · event studies with pre-trend checks · robustness and placebo checks · aggregating and cleaning ~1.2 million community-uploaded runs (DuckDB)

Slay the Spire 2 is a deckbuilder: a game where you build a deck of cards as you play, choosing one new card from a small set offered after most fights. During its early-access period, the developers shipped balance patches that buffed (strengthened), nerfed (weakened), or reworked individual cards. Each change is a small natural experiment.

Spirometry lines those patches up with about 1.2 million community-uploaded runs from Spire Codex and estimates how each change moved a card's pick rate. It uses a difference-in-differences design: the changed card against the same character's unchanged cards, which absorb everything else that shifted between game builds.

Of the first patch's 26 numeric tweaks, 21 clearly moved pick rates within four weeks (median 4.4 percentage points). The second patch's 17 tweaks moved them less than half as much (median 1.9; 8 clearly). Win rates are next.

postdoc · 2020–2026

NASA Roman Space Telescope

How do you design a survey for a telescope that doesn't exist yet?

SkillsLarge-scale simulation (~500 end-to-end survey campaigns) · experimental design under a fixed budget · figure-of-merit comparison · HPC / Slurm

I helped design the High Latitude Time Domain Survey (HLTDS) for NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, a flagship mission that launched in August 2026. The survey design our team recommended beat NASA's dark-energy requirement and became the basis for the survey's observing strategy.

As a postdoc at UC Santa Cruz and a member of one of NASA's Roman Science Investigation Teams, I ran and analyzed about 500 simulated survey campaigns, comparing candidate designs (depth, cadence, filters) on a single figure-of-merit. The survey itself is scheduled to begin in late 2028.

PhD · 2012–2019

Heavy-element origins

Where in the universe is gold made?

SkillsPhysics-based analytical modeling · reanalysis of published observational data

Gold and the other heaviest elements are made by rapid neutron capture, but where in the universe that happens is still debated. I built a physics-based analytical model and used it to reanalyze existing measurements of heavy elements in old stars.

The reanalysis ruled out two leading candidate sources, including collapsars, and became two first-author papers.
